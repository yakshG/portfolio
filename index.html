BACKGROUND

I like taking a mechanic apart and figuring out the system underneath it - that's more or less what got me into this. I started with zero game development experience: Kamikaze was my introduction to Blueprints in my first year of MFA, and by that summer I'd taught myself C++ well enough to build something more ambitious with it.

Physics and astronomy are what pull me in the most, and that's where Our Last Sync came from - two robots with paired black-hole and white-hole abilities, built to make a physics idea feel like something you're doing with a controller instead of just watching. That same pull led me to write my MFA thesis on profiling Our Last Sync's performance across CPU, GPU, and networking in Unreal Engine 5.5. It's also why I keep working through things on my own, like OpenGL and an engine-building series, just to see how an engine gets built from scratch.

I also teach. I've run Unreal Engine workshops for students at Summer Game Studio, supported a university rigging course, and helped design gamified learning experiences for middle schoolers. Explaining something to someone else is usually how I find out if I actually understood it.

I have a B.Tech in Computer Science Engineering and an MFA in Interactive Media from Clark University. Outside of Unreal, I'm usually losing at chess, on a racquetball court, or putting too many hours into Valorant. I'm looking for opportunities to build real systems with people who genuinely care about how things are made.
