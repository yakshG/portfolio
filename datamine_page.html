OVERVIEW

A 2.5D platformer where you play as a data scientist trapped in an excel spreadsheet. Navigate rows and columns, physically mine computers for data, and manage your deteriorating health through an in-game economy. The game explores the human perspective of data mining work.

Developed as a part of a fellowship program with bi-weekly feedback meetings focused on overall project.

WHAT I BUILT

Core Gameplay Systems

  • Mining mechanic with line trace hit detection during attack animation
  • Damageable computer objects that yield data on destruction
  • Economy system balancing health decay, energy consumption, money earned from data sales
  • Store interface (accessed anytime, pauses gameplay) for purchasing health boosts and energy refills

Player Systems

  • 2.5D side-scrolling movement with fixed camera angle
  • Animation montages with notifies to prevent attack button mashing
  • Integrated free 3D assets for walk, run, jump, and attack animations

Weapons & Power-Ups

  • Base weapon system extended for power-up pickups that replace the pistol slot
  • Reviewed and integrated teammate implementations of physics-altering effects (size scaling, anti-gravity, slippery movement)

UI & Polish

  • Main Menu, in-game store, and death/restart screens
  • Dialogue system triggered at specific cells for narrative context
  • Sound effects for mining actions and background music

CHALLENGES

Challenge: Economy balancing

Finding the right balance between health decay rate, data income, and store prices for optimal player experience. Extensive solo iteration and playtesting with friends to avoid making the game too punishing or too easy.

Challenge: Conceptualization

Starting from scratch with only a broad theme (human perspective on mining/data mining) required research and iteration to land on a cohesive game concept that connected both ideas.

WHAT I LEARNED

  • End-to-end solo game development: concept, design, implementation, and release
  • Balancing interconnected economy systems through playtesting feedback
  • Using visual metaphors (spreadsheet structure) to reinforce narrative themes
  • Iterative design process with external feedback from instructors
