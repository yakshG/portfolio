OVERVIEW

A 2.5D platformer where you play as a data scientist trapped in an Excel spreadsheet. Navigate rows and columns, physically mine computers for data, and manage your deteriorating health through an in-game economy. The game explores the human cost of mining and data mining.

Developed as a solo part of a fellowship project with bi-weekly feedback meetings focused on overall project direction and design iteration.

WHAT I BUILT

Core Gameplay Systems

  • Mining mechanic using line trace hit detection during attack animation to detect computer objects.
  • Damageable computer objects with health values that yield data on destruction.
  • Energy cost system: each mining hit consumes energy, requiring strategic resource management.

Economy & Resource Management

  • Four interconnected resource systems:
    • Health - constantly decaying, death occurs at zero
    • Energy - consumed per mining hit, required to mine
    • Data - collected from destroyed computers
    • Money - earned by selling data, used to purchase health and energy
  • Real-time UI updates for all resource values.
  • Balanced economy through iterative playtesting to ensure optimal difficulty curve.

Player Movement & Animation

  • 2.5D side scrolling movement: player moves left/right on a 2D plane with 3D graphics.
  • Animation montages with notifies to prevent attack button mashing and control mining timing.
  • Integrated free 3D assets from Mixamo with custom walk, run, jump, and attack animations.

Level Design & Narrative

  • Designed levels using spreadsheet visual metaphor: grid-based layout representing Excel cells.
  • One-way progression system.
  • Dialogue system triggered at specific cells to provide narrative context.

UI & Polish

  • Main Menu, win & death UI with restart functionality.
  • In-game HUD displaying all four resources.
  • Added free ambient background music & sound effects for mining action.

CHALLENGES

Challenge: Economy balancing

Problem: Finding the right balance between health decay rate, data income, energy costs and store prices to create an engaging challenge without frustrating players.

Solution: Iterated on resource values through repeated playtesting sessions. Final balance ensures players must strategically manage mining vs. purchasing to survive.

Challenge: Conceptualization

Problem: Starting from scratch with only a broad theme (human perspective on mining/data mining) required research and iteration to land on a cohesive game concept connecting both ideas.

Solution: Conducted research on data mining labor conditions, experimented with different mechanical metaphors, and iterated on prototypes. The spreadsheet visual metaphor emerged as the strongest connection between physical labor and data work.

WHAT I LEARNED

  • End-to-end solo game development: concept ideation, design, implementation, playtesting and release.
  • Balancing interconnected economy systems through data-driven playtesting.
  • Using visual metaphors (spreadsheet structure) to reinforce narrative themes mechanically.
  • Iterative design process with external feedback from instructors.
